## Panel-specific overlay

The transformed design is one short, instantly readable phrase that names the photograph's hidden metaphor, hand-lettered very large across the middle in thick black marker — wobbly, uneven in size and stroke weight, written carefully yet never neatly — with clear warm-white space on both sides so no letter touches an edge. Background, light and perspective are deleted; the subject and only two or three small things from the photo become clumsy, chunky doodles (stick legs, dot eyes, a thin black outline with flat matte colour often spilling past it) that lean on, sit on or peek out from behind the strokes, covering only a little of the letters, scattered at different heights. A slanted, unclosed thin-line quadrilateral loosely rings the group with one corner poking past the words, and two tiny slanted handwritten footnotes sit nearby. Black is reserved for the lettering and lines; each doodle takes one bright colour from the photograph; warm white fills most of the half, with generous headroom above, and nothing touches the fold. When the photograph is a scene, doodle only its most representative single object or fragment. Reject a full-scene copy, a filled background, tidy print-like or shrunken lettering, doodles that do not interact with the words, skilled illustration lines, realistic proportions, too many doodles, large colour fields, gradients and greyish colour.

These overlay rules add to `references/soldier-runtime.md`. They never replace the source brief or the family runtime contract.

Write every selected final PNG directly inside one fresh task directory under `~/Desktop/xxd/xxd-panel-351/` (or the explicit `--out` root). Use collision-safe filenames; do not create source, mode, or size subdirectories and do not generate an automatic contact sheet.
